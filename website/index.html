When text becomes cheap, what sustains trust?

A reproducible study of how generative AI may alter provenance, governance, and knowledge production on crowdsourced music-information platforms—using Rate Your Music and Album of the Year as motivating cases.

01 · Structural proposition

A shift in the architecture of scarcity

Generative systems reduce the effort required to produce coherent review-like prose. The scarce resources move upstream: toward credible origin, accountable histories, and institutional knowledge of how contributions are weighted.

Textual plausibility is no longer a sufficient signal of effort, experience, or origin.

What the archives can show

Observed archive

Shared rank order, different score calibration

Across 4,102 exact artist–title–year matches, AOTY and RYM user scores correlate at r = 0.910. After rescaling, 87.4% lie within half a point, while the median AOTY score is 0.34 points higher. C01

Attention concentrates; written participation stays thin

The AOTY top-5,000 file has a rating-count Gini of 0.617; the selected RYM file has a Gini of 0.400. In the RYM snapshot, the median review-to-rating ratio is only 1.65%. C02

The most important result is still open.

Scientific restraint is part of the contribution.

01
No causal claim about ChatGPT and platform change

The archives are cross-sectional and contain no repeated submission timestamps.

02
No estimate of AI-review prevalence

The text study uses published critic excerpts and fixed assistant-style controls, not a representative platform sample.

03
No calibrated trust-collapse forecast

Trust, policy, and competitive-position outputs are conditional scenarios determined by stated assumptions.

04
A dated panel is the decisive next dataset

Repeated albums or users, rule-change records, and documented exclusions would make a structural-break test meaningful.
